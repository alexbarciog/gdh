Romania's retail sales volumes grew 0.9% in August compared with the same month last year, according to data from the National Institute of Statistics (INS) published on 6 October. On a working-day-adjusted basis the increase was smaller, at 0.1%, as [RTTNews reported](https://www.rttnews.com/story.aspx?Id=3696828). Either way, it is the first year-on-year rise after twelve consecutive monthly declines: in July, sales were still 6.1% lower than a year earlier.

## Where the growth came from

The recovery is uneven across categories. Compared with August last year:

- Non-food sales rose 2.7%
- Fuel sales rose 2.5%
- Food, beverages and tobacco fell 2.3%

Month on month, the picture was stronger across the board. Total retail sales were up 2.3% on July, after a 0.7% rise the month before. Non-food grew 3.9%, fuel 3.6% and food 1.3%.

## The longer view

August's figure follows a difficult year for Romanian shops. In the first half of 2026, retail sales volumes were 5.6% lower than a year earlier, [ZF English reported](https://www.zfenglish.com/companies/retail-consumer/statistical-office-romania-retail-sales-down-5-6-yoy-in-january-june-2026-23211222) from INS data. Even with August's improvement, sales over the twelve months to August remained 4.7% below the previous twelve months, according to bne IntelliNews.

## Why it matters for brands

One month of growth is not yet a recovery, and the split matters more than the headline. Food volumes are still shrinking year on year, so grocery brands are competing for a smaller basket, and food buyers are likely to stay cautious about new listings and demanding on promotional support. Non-food categories such as household and personal care are where volume is returning first, which makes them the easier conversation with a chain buyer this autumn. September's figures, expected in early November, will show whether August was a turning point or a pause.
